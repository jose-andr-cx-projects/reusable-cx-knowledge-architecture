# Problem Hypotheses

Status: Hypothesis

## Working question

> How might we make important CX knowledge easier to govern, reuse and apply across teams, channels and delivery work without creating more documentation or maintenance overhead?

## Current hypotheses

The working group may be experiencing one or more of the following problems:

### Repeated interpretation

CX principles, standards or intentions may need to be repeatedly translated into project-specific guidance.

### Multiple representations

Similar CX knowledge may appear across different documents, projects, channels or delivery tools.

### Weak strategy-to-delivery translation

CX intent may not always translate consistently into design requirements, implementation decisions or validation criteria.

### Findability and authority

People may have difficulty determining:

- whether relevant guidance already exists;
- which version is current;
- who owns it;
- whether it is mandatory or advisory.

### Maintenance burden

Updating equivalent guidance across multiple locations may create unnecessary reconciliation and maintenance effort.

### Emerging machine-consumption needs

Future AI, search, automation or development workflows may benefit from clearer and more structured organisational knowledge.

This is a secondary hypothesis.

The project should not assume that AI or machine-readable knowledge is required.

## Questions for discovery

1. Where is CX knowledge currently being recreated or reinterpreted?
2. Where do teams experience conflicting guidance?
3. Which knowledge needs to remain consistent across multiple contexts?
4. Where does information become difficult to find or trust?
5. Which handoffs create repeated interpretation?
6. What maintenance activity creates low-value effort?
7. Which issues materially affect customer or staff outcomes?
8. Which problems are significant enough to justify intervention?

## Validation rule

Do not convert a hypothesis into a requirement based only on technical feasibility.

Seek evidence through actual stakeholder and delivery experience.
